When it gets cold, there's less to do with kids, but most children still love water. That makes a hot spring water park the perfect winter outing. Asan, a spa town south of Seoul, pairs a hot spring water park with a stone-walled folk village and a shrine to Korea's most famous admiral. Here's an easy 2-day plan.

Quick facts

  • From Seoul: Seoul Subway Line 1 or the KTX/train to Onyangoncheon Station area, then a taxi; or about 1.5 hours by car
  • Best for: toddlers to primary kids
  • Know before you go: kids 4 and older can't use the opposite-sex changing room

1. Asan Spavis: a hot spring water park

A water park filled with natural hot spring water, with indoor and outdoor hot pools, a wave pool and slides. In winter, kids get a kick out of soaking in a warm outdoor pool with cold air on their faces.

  • Address: 157-29 Asanoncheon-ro, Eumbong-myeon, Asan (Korean: 충남 아산시 음봉면 아산온천로 157-29)
  • Prices (last summer, per a blog): adults about 40,000s won, children about 30,000s won
  • Under 36 months: free with proof of age
  • Hours: blogs disagree, so check the official site

Changing rooms: children aged 4 and older can't use the opposite-sex changing room or showers. If it's mom with a son or dad with a daughter, plan who goes where before you arrive.

Many reviews suggest arriving before 9 AM for parking and lockers. Do the indoor pools in the morning and outdoor pools in the afternoon.

2. Oeam Folk Village: a gentle walk the next day

After a day in the water, kids need an easy walk. Oeam Folk Village keeps old stone walls and thatched and tiled houses. People still live here, so keep your voice down.

  • Best for: early primary and up
  • Tickets: adults 2,000 won, children 1,000 won
  • Open: every day (some facilities close on Mondays) · parking available
  • Strollers: the stone paths can be bumpy

3. Hyeonchungsa Shrine: free history lesson

This shrine honors Admiral Yi Sun-sin, who defeated the Japanese navy in the 1590s with his turtle ships. Primary-school kids may recognize him from Korean money and statues.

  • Admission: free · Closed: Mondays
  • Winter hours (from November): 09:00–17:00, last entry 16:00

The nearby Gokgyocheon ginkgo road is famous, but its peak is early to mid-November, so many leaves may have fallen by late November.

Food and rainy days

Restaurants cluster around the spa area. Kids' menus and high chairs vary. If it rains, the Spavis indoor pools are your backup; skip the slippery village paths and focus on Hyeonchungsa's indoor exhibits.

A 2-day plan

  1. Day 1: arrive at Spavis before 9 AM → indoor pools → lunch → outdoor pools → finish around 3 PM → rest at the hotel
  2. Day 2: Oeam Folk Village (1 hour) → lunch → Hyeonchungsa (1 hour) → home

Kids get much more tired after water play than you'd expect, so keep the first evening free. Take breaks and drink water often in the hot water.

Tips for foreign travelers

  • Pack swim caps, goggles, a waterproof pouch and spare clothes; swim caps are often required at Korean water parks.
  • Show taxi drivers the Korean names: 아산스파비스, 외암민속마을, 현충사.
  • Hyeonchungsa is closed on Mondays.

Summary

Asan shines when it's cold: hot spring water play on day one and an easy village walk on day two. A great late-November family weekend.
