Not up for a long trip, but want some fresh air with the kids? Paju, just north of Seoul, is an easy day out. At the end of November it also hosts the Jangdan Bean Festival, where kids can thresh beans and make little blocks of fermented soybean (meju), a hands-on bit of Korean food culture you rarely get to try.

Quick facts

  • From Seoul: easiest by car; for the festival, take the Gyeongui-Jungang Line to Munsan Station and the free shuttle
  • Bring: your passport for the peace gondola
  • Avoid: Mondays (museum closed) and Sundays for the tofu restaurant

1. 30th Paju Jangdan Bean Festival (Nov 27–29)

The festival takes place at Imjingak Plaza, near the border, with an opening ceremony at 2 PM on day one. Last year's activities included mini meju making, bean threshing and roasting beans; this year's program is not fully out yet.

  • Dates: Friday November 27 – Sunday November 29
  • Place: Imjingak Plaza (Korean: 임진각 광장)
  • Shuttle: free, 09:00–17:00, running regularly from Munsan Station exit 1
  • Parking: Imjingak temporary lot and Peace lot; they fill up fast

2. Imjingak Peace Gondola: bring ID

This gondola crosses the Imjin River into the Civilian Control Zone, so you need ID. For on-site tickets, the group leader shows ID and riders sign a pledge; foreign visitors should carry a passport.

  • Round trip: standard cabin adults 12,000 won, children 10,000 won; Crystal cabin adults 15,000 won, children 13,000 won; under 36 months free
  • Hours: 09:00–18:00 (last entry 17:30), closed on maintenance days
  • Parking: 2,000 won

3. Majang Lake suspension bridge: free, but not for strollers

A bouncy bridge across the lake that kids love. Entry is free and parking lot 6 is free too. There are lots of stairs, so bring a baby carrier instead of a stroller.

  • Nov–Feb hours: 09:00–17:00, open daily
  • Parking: lot 6 free; other lots 10 minutes free, then 2,000 won for 24 hours
  • Nursing room: near parking lot 2

It closes at 5 PM in November, so go early in the afternoon.

4. Rainy day: National Folk Museum of Korea, Paju

A free museum built around its storage: you can walk into the "open storage" and look into the "visible storage" through glass. Shelves packed with old objects look like a treasure room to kids.

  • Admission: free · Hours: 10:00–18:00 (last entry 17:30)
  • Closed: Mondays (Tuesday if Monday is a holiday), January 1, Lunar New Year and Chuseok days
  • Booking: (nfm.go.kr/paju)

In the evening, First Garden lights up every night after dark. Weekdays adults 10,000 won, children 9,000 won.

5. Lunch: Jangdan bean tofu

Paju's Jangdan beans make soft, mild tofu that kids eat easily.

  • Paju Jangdan Kong Dubu (파주장단콩두부): 717 Tongil-ro, 11:00–20:00 (break 16:00–17:00), parking for 30 cars, closed Sundays

The last festival day is a Sunday, when this place is closed, so plan another lunch spot that day.

A one-day plan

  1. 10:00 National Folk Museum, Paju (start indoors)
  2. 12:00 Tofu lunch
  3. 13:30 Majang Lake bridge (leave before 5 PM)
  4. 17:30 First Garden lights → head home
  5. Festival week (Nov 27–29): Bean Festival in the morning → peace gondola → home

Tips for foreign travelers

  • Places near the border check ID; always keep your passport on you.
  • Use Naver Map or KakaoMap; a T-money card works on the subway to Munsan.
  • Renting a car? You need an International Driving Permit.

Summary

Free museum, tofu, the lake bridge and garden lights, plus the Bean Festival in its final week of November. Paju is close and cheap, an ideal weekend outing with kids.
