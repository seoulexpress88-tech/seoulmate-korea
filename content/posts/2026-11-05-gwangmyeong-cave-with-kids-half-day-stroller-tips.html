Looking for something close to Seoul that works even when it's cold or raining? Gwangmyeong Cave is a former gold mine that has been turned into a themed attraction, with an aquarium, a "golden waterfall" and glowing walkways inside. It suits a relaxed half-day outing, and this guide covers the practical bits parents ask about: temperature, strollers, parking and pacing.

Quick facts

  • Address: 142 Gahak-ro 85beon-gil, Gwangmyeong, Gyeonggi-do (Korean: 경기 광명시 가학로85번길 142)
  • Hours: 09:00–18:00, last entry 17:00
  • Closed: every Monday
  • Admission: adults 10,000 won · teens 5,000 won · children 3,000 won
  • Phone: 070-4277-8902 (Korean-language line)
  • From Seoul: Gwangmyeong is just southwest of Seoul, a short trip by subway, KTX/train or car
  • Visit length: about 1 to 1.5 hours; up to nearly 2 hours if you take lots of photos

The cave is cool: 12–15°C all year

Inside, the temperature stays at roughly 12–15°C year-round. In November that can feel similar to outside or even a bit warmer, but some sections have wet floors. Bring a light jacket and non-slip sneakers, not sandals or smooth-soled shoes.

What children like

  • Cave Aqua World: an aquarium built inside the cave
  • Golden Path and Golden Waterfall: sparkly spots for photos
  • Cave garden and mushroom-growing area
  • Cave Palace of Arts: a space that screens media art
  • Wine Cave: for adults; tasting is for adults only

The sweet spot is children who can walk well, roughly age 5 through elementary school. Younger kids can enjoy it too, but the dark interior surprises some babies and toddlers, so keep that visit short.

Can you take a stroller?

The short answer: possible, but not comfortable. There are flat stretches, but slopes and stairs are mixed in.

  • Stroller rental at the entrance has been mentioned
  • On steeper parts, you may need to lift the stroller together
  • For babies around one year old, a baby carrier makes the visit much easier
  • Ask at the ticket office about the nursing room

Parking

  • Parking Lot 1 (Gahak-dong 10-4) is the closest to the cave
  • Fees: small and medium cars 3,000 won, compact cars 1,500 won, large vehicles 4,000 won; pay at the entrance in advance
  • Weekends get busy, so arrive early; if Lot 1 is full, staff direct you to Lots 2 and 3

A relaxed half-day plan

  • 09:30 – Arrive at Parking Lot 1
  • 10:00 – Enter the cave (about 1.5 hours)
  • 11:30 – Snack and rest outside
  • 12:00 – Lunch, then head back

Finishing in the morning leaves the afternoon free for naps. There is said to be a food court and café outside the cave; check what's on offer on site.

Packing by age

  • Ages 0–2: baby carrier, a handkerchief, spare socks. The dark cave can startle babies, so keep it short.
  • Ages 3–6: hold hands the whole way, and save snacks for outside.
  • Elementary school: telling the gold-mining story beforehand makes the visit more fun.

Children often tire out sooner than expected after the cave. On a rainy day, you only need an umbrella for the walk between the parking lot and the entrance.

Tips for foreign travelers

  • Use Naver Map or KakaoMap and search the Korean name: 광명동굴. Show it to a taxi driver if you take one from the station.
  • Check the admission price on the city website (gm.go.kr) before you go, since resident discounts can confuse the listed price.
  • Remember it is closed on Mondays.
  • Pay the parking fee at the entrance if you drive; check payment methods on site.

Summary

A cool, dry, easy-to-reach cave with an aquarium and glowing paths: Gwangmyeong Cave is a good indoor-style pick close to Seoul. Wear non-slip shoes, take a carrier for babies, arrive early, and plan the afternoon off.
