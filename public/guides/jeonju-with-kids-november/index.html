Jeonju with Kids: 5 Family-Friendly Spots in November (Free Museum, Zoo & Rainy-Day Options)

Oct 29, 20262 min read
Prices, opening hours and transit times change often. We last checked them on Oct 29, 2026. Please confirm on the official site or Naver Map before you go.

Jeonju is famous for its hanok village and bibimbap, but it's also an easy, affordable city for families. It's about 1 hour 40 minutes from Seoul by KTX, so it works as a day trip or a relaxed overnight.

This guide picks five places with kids in mind: stroller access, baby facilities and somewhere to go if it rains.

Quick facts

  • From Seoul: KTX to Jeonju Station, about 1h 40m–2h
  • Best for: preschool to early elementary ages
  • Budget: most places cost 1,000–3,000 won for kids, and the national museum is free
  • Autumn colors: forecast to peak in early November around Jeonju

1. National Jeonju Museum Children's Museum (free)

Start here. Entry is free, individual visitors don't need a reservation, and the museum opens on Mondays, when many attractions close. A parent must stay with children.

  • Address: 249 Ssukgogae-ro, Wansan-gu, Jeonju (Korean: 전북 전주시 완산구 쑥고개로 249)
  • Hours: Children's Museum 10:00–17:30; main galleries 10:00–18:00
  • Breaks: the children's area closes for cleaning 12:00–13:00 and 15:00–15:30 daily
  • Tip: arrive at 10:00, play until noon, then go for lunch.

2. Jeonju Zoo

One of Korea's cheapest zoos: adults 3,000 won, children 1,000 won, under 5 free. Strollers for babies under 24 months can be borrowed near the lost-child center. The zoo is big, so pick three or four animals you really want to see.

  • Parking: 1,500 won · open every day
  • November hours: check before you go

3. Jeonju Hanok Village & Gyeonggijeon Shrine

The tourist information center has a nursing room, a diaper-changing table and stroller rental. Some side alleys are bumpy, so stick to the main streets with a stroller.

  • Gyeonggijeon: adults 3,000 won, children 1,000 won (includes the Royal Portrait Museum)
  • Note: the main shrine hall is under repainting work until about December 17, so that area is closed
  • Public parking: 1,200 won for the first 30 minutes
  • Event: on Saturday, November 21, a reenactment of a 1593 royal exam parade runs to Gyeonggijeon, with free crafts and traditional snacks.

4. Rainy-day backup: Jeonju Natural Ecology Museum

A small indoor nature museum with a nursing room and high chairs. Open Tuesday–Sunday 09:00–18:00, closed Mondays. Adults about 2,000 won, children 500 won, 6 and under free.

5. Kid-friendly lunch: tteokgalbi

Children often prefer tteokgalbi (sweet, tender grilled short-rib patties) to spicy bibimbap. Gyodong Tteokgalbi near the hanok village is said to have high chairs; it has no parking, so use the public lot.

  • 52 Eunhaeng-ro, Wansan-gu, Jeonju (전주시 완산구 은행로 52) · 11:00–20:30 · tteokgalbi for two about 30,000 won

A half-day plan

  1. 10:00 Children's Museum
  2. 12:00 Tteokgalbi lunch near the hanok village (15–20 minutes by car)
  3. 13:30 Gyeonggijeon and a main-street walk
  4. 15:30 Café break, then the zoo if energy remains

Tips for foreign travelers

  • Use Naver Map or KakaoMap; local buses and walking routes are much more accurate there.
  • Taxis are cheap in Jeonju. Show the Korean address to the driver.
  • Hanbok rental shops line the village; kids' sizes are common.

Summary

Free children's museum, tteokgalbi lunch, then a hanok village stroll: that's an easy family day in Jeonju. On rainy days, swap in the ecology museum; on sunny days, add the zoo.
