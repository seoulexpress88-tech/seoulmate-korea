Busan Fireworks Festival 2026 (Nov 7): Best Viewing Spots, Tickets & How to Get There

Oct 28, 20264 min read
Prices, opening hours and transit times change often. We last checked them on Oct 28, 2026. Please confirm on the official site or Naver Map before you go.

Once a year, Gwangan Bridge in Busan turns into a stage for one of Asia's biggest fireworks shows. Hundreds of thousands of people fill Gwangalli Beach and the coastline around it to watch.

The 21st Busan Fireworks Festival takes place on Saturday, November 7, 2026. This guide covers the schedule, paid seats, the best free viewing spots, how to get there from Seoul, and what foreign visitors should know before going.

Quick facts

  • Date: Saturday, November 7, 2026
  • Main show: 7:00–8:00 PM, with a short intermission around 7:15–7:25 PM
  • Where: Gwangalli Beach, Igidae and Dongbaekseom Island, all facing Gwangan Bridge
  • Cost: free to watch from public areas; paid seats on the beach are 70,000–100,000 won
  • Getting there: Busan Metro Line 2. There is no event parking, so don't drive.

The show schedule

The evening is split into three parts. It opens with a show by an invited international fireworks team (about 7:00–7:15 PM). After a short break comes the main Busan Multi Fireworks Show (about 7:25–7:55 PM), choreographed to music with fireworks fired from the bridge itself. A short curtain-call display closes the night around 8:00 PM.

Daytime events on the beach start in the afternoon. Ticket holders can enter from 3:00 PM.

Paid seats vs. free viewing

Official paid seats sit right on the sand at Gwangalli Beach, facing the center of the bridge:

  • R seat: a table and chair, 100,000 won
  • S seat: a chair, 70,000 won

Tickets are sold online through NOL (Yanolja) until 5:00 PM on November 6. Paid-seat holders must reach their seats before 6:00 PM; latecomers can't enter and can't get a refund.

Is a paid seat worth it? If you want a guaranteed front view without arriving at noon, yes. If you're fine with crowds and a side angle, the free spots below work well.

5 places to watch the fireworks

1. Gwangalli Beach (free sections)

The closest and loudest view. Free sections of the beach fill up from early afternoon on the day, so bring a picnic mat and arrive by 1–2 PM if you want sand to sit on.

  • Address: 219 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro, Suyeong-gu, Busan (Korean: 부산 수영구 광안해변로 219)
  • Tip: the east end near Millak-dong is usually a little less packed than the center.

2. Igidae Coastal Walk

Igidae is one of the official event areas. Its cliffs face Gwangan Bridge from the south, so you see the bridge and the Haeundae skyline behind the fireworks. The walk is uneven and dark at night, so wear proper shoes and bring a phone light.

  • Area: Igidae Park, Nam-gu, Busan (Korean: 부산 남구 이기대공원)

3. Dongbaekseom Island & APEC House

The other official event area, on the Haeundae side. You watch the bridge from the north-east, with a calmer, park-like setting. A good choice if you're staying in Haeundae.

  • Area: Dongbaekseom, Haeundae-gu, Busan (Korean: 부산 해운대구 동백섬)

4. Hwangnyeongsan Mountain

For a high, wide view of the whole bridge and city, locals drive or hike up Hwangnyeongsan. Roads up the mountain can be restricted on festival day, and it gets cold after sunset in November.

5. A room or café with a bridge view

Hotels and cafés facing Gwangalli sell out months ahead and often raise prices for this night. Busan City warns visitors to check booking terms carefully, so book directly and keep your confirmation.

How to get there

  • From Seoul: KTX from Seoul Station to Busan Station takes about 2 hours 40 minutes. Book early; trains on the festival weekend fill up.
  • From Busan Station: take Metro Line 1 to Seomyeon, then Line 2 toward Jangsan. Get off at Geumnyeonsan or Gwangan Station and walk about 10 minutes to the beach.
  • Heads-up: nearby stations can get extremely crowded, and trains sometimes skip stops for safety on the night. Consider walking from Suyeong or Namcheon Station.
  • Leaving: expect 1–2 hours for crowds to clear. Many people stay for dinner nearby instead of rushing to the subway.

A simple plan for the day

  1. Late morning: arrive in Busan and drop your bags.
  2. Lunch: eat early, because restaurants near the beach get busy after 2 PM.
  3. 1–3 PM: claim a free spot, or explore and arrive at your paid seat between 3 and 6 PM.
  4. 7–8 PM: the show.
  5. After 8 PM: late seafood dinner in Millak-dong while the crowds thin out.

Where to eat

The Millak-dong area at the east end of Gwangalli is known for its raw fish market and seafood restaurants. Pick your fish downstairs and eat it upstairs or at a nearby restaurant. On festival night, expect queues and a limited menu. Convenience stores along the beach are the easiest option for snacks and hot drinks.

Tips for foreign travelers

  • Dress warmly. Early November evenings by the sea are cold, often around 10°C. Bring a jacket, a blanket and a mat.
  • Use Naver Map or KakaoMap for subway and walking routes. Google Maps' walking directions in Korea are limited.
  • Load your T-money card before the day so you don't queue at ticket machines.
  • Agree on a meeting point. Mobile networks can slow down in the crowd.
  • Follow staff and police directions. Crowd-control lines move people in one direction after the show.
  • Rain or wind can change the program, so check the official site (busanfireworks.com) on the day.

Summary

Busan Fireworks Festival 2026 is on Saturday, November 7, 7–8 PM at Gwangalli. Buy a paid seat for the easiest front view, or arrive early for free spots on the beach, Igidae or Dongbaekseom. Take the subway, dress warmly, and plan to stay for a late dinner while the crowds clear.
